How much does a security door cost? A guide to prices and details

A door to your security

When it comes to home security, your front door is the first line between peace of mind and risk. That is why choosing a security door is not just a purchase, but a real investment in protecting your home and your loved ones.

But how much does a security front door really cost? And what affects the final price? In this guide we take you step by step through costs, technical features, tax deductions and good practice, so you can make an informed choice.

Why choose a security door?

It is not only about burglaries, but also about everyday wellbeing. Knowing you are protected helps you live better and with more peace of mind, especially in cities or in large apartment blocks.

As Vera Begnis, showroom manager at Infissi Group, explains: “Today a security door is much more than an anti-burglary product: it is a design feature, a thermal and acoustic insulator, and a calling card that has to combine security and beauty.”

Price ranges: how much does it cost?

Security door prices depend on a great many factors, but we can identify a few indicative price bands:

  • Entry level: ideal for flats inside a building (off a shared landing) or for basic needs
  • Mid-range: for those looking for a good balance between design and performance
  • Top of the range: for made-to-measure solutions, premium finishes and very high performance

On top of the price of the door you choose, you need to add the installation costs, which vary depending on the home (for example whether there is a subframe, whether the old door has to be removed, and so on).

What affects the cost?

At first glance one security door may look just like another, but behind it lies a whole world of technical differences. Here are some of the key factors that affect the price:

  1. Security class

Security doors are classified from 1 to 6 under the UNI EN 1627 standard. For homes, class 3 is the recommended minimum, while class 4 offers greater protection against more sophisticated break-in attempts.

“There is no point choosing a class 2 security door today”, says Vera. “People who invest in security want a door that really acts as a deterrent.”

  1. Locks and locking mechanisms

Euro profile cylinders with anti-drill defenders, electronic locks, keys with copy protection... every upgrade adds to the cost, but also to the protection. It is often worth spending a little more to avoid nasty surprises.

  1. Looks, finishes and customisation

Security doors are no longer anonymous slabs of metal. You can have:

  • Wood, porcelain stoneware, aluminium or laminate cladding
  • Decorative routed patterns
  • Custom colours
  • Security glass inserts

And if you want a look that matches your interior doors, you can ask for coordinated finishes, for a wow factor that enhances not only security but also the harmony of your rooms. From natural wood species to more modern shades, a security door can become a real design feature.
